About

I am a research scientist at Oak Ridge National Laboratory. My path runs from the laboratory bench to the quantum computing: I began by fabricating functional surfaces and membranes, moved to data-driven design of thermal and photonic materials, and now build AI-quantum-HPC algorithms and workflows.

My long-term goal is computational discovery of materials and devices with tailored thermal, optical, and energy-conversion properties.

Research

Designing a functional material means choosing composition, geometry, and structure all at once. The number of candidates grows exponentially, the choices are discrete and strongly coupled, and every candidate is expensive to evaluate by simulation or experiment.

Classical optimization struggles as these problems become large and dense. I treat AI, quantum computing, and high-performance computing as one workflow for searching them. Classical optimization struggles as these problems become large and dense, and that is where I apply the three technologies as one workflow.

How the three work together

  • Quantum computingSearches design spaces that overwhelm classical solversI develop distributed quantum optimization algorithms that break large, densely coupled, higher-order problems into pieces today’s quantum processors can run.
  • Artificial intelligenceLearns from scarce, costly dataSurrogate models and active learning decide which candidates are worth evaluating next, and generative models write quantum circuits for problems they have not seen before.
  • High-performance computingMakes the workflow scaleSupercomputers coordinate problem decomposition, quantum and GPU execution, and high-fidelity simulation, so one loop runs from a design question to a validated answer.
