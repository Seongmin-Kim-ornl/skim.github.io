About

I develop inverse design methods, generative quantum solvers, and distributed optimization frameworks that connect materials modeling with high-fidelity simulation and experimental validation.

My path runs from the laboratory bench to the supercomputer. I began by fabricating functional surfaces and membranes, moved to data-driven inverse design of thermal and photonic materials, and now build quantum-HPC systems at Oak Ridge. The long-term goal is computational design of materials and devices with tailored thermal, optical, and energy-conversion properties.

Research

Functional materials are designed through coupled choices in composition, geometry, topology, and processing. Those design spaces are high-dimensional, part discrete and part continuous, and expensive to evaluate.

My central question is how advanced computing can expand the class of materials and engineering problems we can solve without giving up physical rigor. I treat AI, quantum computing, and HPC as three stages of one discovery loop, and I measure progress by scientific time-to-discovery: the total cost in data, optimization, computation, and validation of reaching a design you can trust.

1

Learn

How do we learn structure-property landscapes when every simulation or experiment is expensive?

Surrogate models, active learning, and inverse design that pick the most informative candidates, tested on thermal-photonic materials and architected metamaterials.

2

Search

How do we optimize design spaces too large for a single quantum algorithm?

Distributed quantum optimization that decomposes dense, higher-order problems across CPUs, GPUs, and quantum processors, with generative AI writing the circuits.

3

Trust

When does error-corrected quantum computing change what materials we can predict?

Fault-tolerant workflows that tie error correction and resource estimates directly to the accuracy of materials and molecular calculations.

Validation by high-fidelity simulation or experiment closes the loop and feeds the next round of learning.
